01. Tesla: Controls engineering intern

May to August 2026, Giga Texas, Austin. Category: tesla.

A summer as a controls engineering intern at Giga Texas, in three parts: PLC and HMI software for production equipment, a local AI and knowledge toolkit that helps find faults faster, and electrical root-cause work on the line that starts from the drawings.

  • Software and controls, PLC and HMI applications: I developed and commissioned Siemens TIA Portal applications in SCL, ladder and function block diagram, on S7-1500 standard and fail-safe CPUs, including the HMI tag models and operator workflows for production equipment.
  • Software and controls, Reusable interfaces: Reusable PLC and HMI interfaces for dock-side indication, safety reintegration acknowledge paths, process interlocks and multi-axis debounce timing, each checked online with watch tables before handoff.
  • Software and controls, Badge and RFID sign-in: Badge and RFID sign-in for production HMIs: normalizing credentials, handling the REST requests and responses, the SSO and tap-login paths, and fixing mismatched tags, scripts and endpoints.
  • Software and controls, Reading the old binaries: I reverse-engineered legacy authentication helper binaries into readable control flow, mapped the identity chain against the Siemens project structure, and built a Control Center integration for this site without carrying over another plant's configuration.
  • Software and controls, Interface specifications: PLC-HMI interface specifications that separate direct writes from cyclic abstraction layers, and an inventory of plant HMI and IPC addresses used across several production lines.
  • AI systems and knowledge tooling, A local model behind human review: A local inference workflow over a private controls corpus, for SCL and HMI authoring, fault classification and procedure routing. Nothing the model produced reached a PLC or a safety change without human review.
  • AI systems and knowledge tooling, A second brain for controls work: A knowledge system in structured Markdown, with wikilinks, frontmatter metadata and an automatic graph rebuild that links assets, blocks, faults and procedures, so a question can follow several hops.
  • AI systems and knowledge tooling, Skills written as contracts: Agent skills as versioned Markdown contracts, each with preconditions, inputs, steps, acceptance checks and failure modes: tracing a schematic, proving out a watch table, auditing HMI tag paths and filing a post-incident report.
  • AI systems and knowledge tooling, Tuned on real debug traces: On labeled debug traces (software versus power or network faults, unscanned call trees, credential parse errors) I tuned the chunking, link density and skill wording until retrieval came back with the actual fix path.
  • AI systems and knowledge tooling, Used on live incidents: On production incidents the graph and skills led the next step from the schematic net to the diagnostics, the tag and the call tree, which cut the repeat time to isolate pendant recovery faults, intermittent dropouts and badge API faults.
  • AI systems and knowledge tooling, Data stays local: No plant data went into external training. The model and skills were limited to authoring, navigation and running runbooks.
  • Electrical, schematics and root cause, Starting from the drawings: I read electrical schematics and panel drawings to trace power distribution, industrial network segments and safety circuits, and checked the hardware against the drawing package rather than guessing from the software alone.
  • Electrical, schematics and root cause, Test cells: I wired and commissioned multi-device test cells (PLC, remote I/O, HMI, pendant dock and safety gate lock), separated power and data cabling faults from application logic, and wrote up how to tell them apart for the team.
  • Electrical, schematics and root cause, Intermittent faults: I led the isolation of intermittent faults on major production lines by lining up bench baselines, PLC diagnostic buffers and the cable, switch and power paths from the schematics, and turned down raising watchdogs or timeouts as the fix.
  • Electrical, schematics and root cause, Pendants that recover: I restored safety reintegration on the handheld operator pendants after a power loss, so they recovered from an HMI acknowledge without a full PLC download. The causes were acknowledge tags that weren't bound and post-safety logic left out of the cyclic call tree.
  • Electrical, schematics and root cause, Torque, debounce and an interlock: I fixed torque-limit selection so the at-speed limits latch after acceleration, put one HMI-adjustable debounce across several axes, and staged a cross-program process interlock that stays disabled until its setpoints pass safety validation.

Tools: Siemens TIA Portal, S7-1500 standard and fail-safe, SCL / LAD / FBD, HMI, Watch tables, RFID, REST, SSO, Local LLM inference, Markdown knowledge graph, Electrical schematics, Remote I/O, Industrial networks.

Role: controls engineering, plc / hmi, ai tooling, root cause.

03. 4D Mapping: Object detection and AI optimization

2026, Personal project. Category: ai.

A plain 2D video goes in. Object detection finds the subject in every frame, depth models and camera solving lift each frame into 3D, and the frames are joined into one scene you can orbit and scrub through time. Earlier positions stay in the scene as ghosts, so a deer's whole path through the woods shows at once. The demo below comes out of my own pipeline from a single clip, with no 3D scanner and no extra cameras.

  • Space and time from one video: One ordinary video is enough: the camera's path is solved, the subject is tracked frame by frame, and the whole scene is placed in x, y, z and time.
  • Detection, measured: On a 1,000-image hold-out set, RF-DETR large finds people at 0.878 AP@0.50 in 35 ms a frame on an M5 Max, against 0.489 for the small model that runs on the device.
  • What the stock detectors miss: For subjects such as a deer, open-vocabulary detection and segmentation (Grounding DINO and SAM) find them and cut them out.
  • The photoreal version: Gaussian splats and per-frame 3D Gaussians, trained on the real frames and scored on frames held out from training.
  • No one in the loop: Drop a clip in a folder and the pipeline runs end to end and writes its own report.
  • Its own app: A native desktop app in Rust on wgpu: a 4D timeline, orbit, top-down and follow cameras, and manual correction of detections.

Tools: Python, PyTorch (Apple MPS), RF-DETR, Grounding DINO, SAM, Depth Anything 3, COLMAP, Gaussian splatting, Rust, wgpu, Core ML.

Role: computer vision, ai, software.

05. ThinkFree Finance: Founder and software engineer

April 2025 to February 2026, College Station, Texas. Category: ai.

ThinkFree Finance explains markets for people who don't follow them for a living: renters, hourly workers, small-business owners, students. It reads the news, links it to public records and writes plain-English reports. A language model can state a wrong number as confidently as a right one, so the pipeline checks what it can and labels what it can't.

  • The news pipeline: News is collected, clustered by embeddings and summarized by a language model, with FAISS retrieval over economic reference texts; it's built to process over 1,000 articles a day.
  • Following the money: It links politicians, bills, lobbying, congressional trades and public spending, using SEC EDGAR, OpenFEC, USASpending, BLS, Census and other public sources.
  • Checking the numbers: Numbers in generated reports are checked against the source data, and a summary that fails the check is withheld.
  • Website and app: A FastAPI website with accounts, MFA, CSRF protection, role-based access and a hash-chained audit log, plus a SwiftUI iPhone app.
  • My own security review: In October 2026 I ran my own security review and logged every bug I found with the test that proves the fix.

Tools: Python, FastAPI, FAISS, Sentence embeddings, LLM APIs, JavaScript, SwiftUI, pytest.

Role: founder, software, ai.

ThinkFree Finance: github / thinkfreefinance

06. hardcaml-pwm: Digital hardware design in OCaml

October 2026. Category: engineering.

A small piece of digital hardware written in OCaml with Jane Street's Hardcaml. Turn a rotary encoder and a PWM output follows, sliding to the new duty cycle over a few periods instead of jumping there. Simulated and tested, with the Verilog generated; a learning project that hasn't been run on an FPGA board yet.

  • A PWM that glides: Each new duty-cycle target ramps in over a fixed number of periods and lands exactly, with no rounding error.
  • Reading the encoder: A quadrature decoder for an EC11-style rotary encoder: synchronizers, debouncing, direction from the A and B edges, and a click only on a real detent.
  • Tested in simulation: Every block has waveform tests in simulation, and the Verilog is generated from the OCaml.

Tools: OCaml, Hardcaml, Verilog, Simulation, Expect tests.

Role: digital design, ocaml.

hardcaml-pwm: github / hardcaml-pwm
